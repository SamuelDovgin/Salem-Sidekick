# Catalyst: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: Town Outlier.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

An Outlier that Overcharges a player to enable additional actions on the following ability cycle. It is Unique and needs a compatible setup. Town Outlier.

**Expanded copy — same section**

An Outlier that Overcharges a player to enable additional actions on the following ability cycle. It is Unique and needs a compatible setup.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is Town Outlier. Use Town Outlier or your exact role when the setup calls for it; do not invent a universal acronym for an Outlier.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Overcharge an eligible player only if you have a reason to make their following ability cycle stronger. This is setup-dependent Town Outlier play. Prefer a supported ally whose particular Overcharge effect you understand.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Overcharge an eligible player only if you have a reason to make their following ability cycle stronger. This is setup-dependent Town Outlier play.

**How to choose:** Prefer a supported ally whose particular Overcharge effect you understand. With no reliable claim, holding is safer than strengthening an unknown attacker. Read the recipient role’s Catalyst interaction before promising a benefit.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Identify a supported recipient. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record the selected player and actual feedback.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record the selected player and actual feedback. Do not assume an effect happened during the placement night; timing depends on the recipient’s ability and the following cycle.

**What this does and does not establish:** More actions can explain unusual visit counts. An extra action is not automatic proof of a specific role.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Use the recipient’s actual reported effect to decide whether to continue or choose another supported ally.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Use the recipient’s actual reported effect to decide whether to continue or choose another supported ally. If the role interaction is uncertain, preserve that uncertainty and avoid a made-up timing guarantee.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Verify the setup and find a trustworthy useful recipient.
- **Middle:** Coordinate extra actions with the actual recipient’s ability rules.
- **Late:** Concentrate value on a remaining action that can change the outcome.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not assume it can spawn from any normal bucket. Do not promise every role behaves identically. Do not use a guessed same-night timing rule.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not assume it can spawn from any normal bucket. Do not promise every role behaves identically. Do not use a guessed same-night timing rule.

**Important interaction:** Role-specific Overcharge details vary; several have official bug fixes. Complex day/night timing stays in advanced notes until client checked.

**Worked decision:** An established investigative ally can use another action next cycle: coordinate the benefit and log the charge application.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Identify a supported recipient.
2. Understand that role’s Overcharge.
3. Confirm timing through actual feedback.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Identify a supported recipient.

- Confirm the role can exist in this setup.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

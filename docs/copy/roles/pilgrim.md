## Your role and immediate job

An Outlier Town role with no active ability. Its value comes from discussion, voting, and information organization rather than nightly feedback. Town Outlier.

**Expanded copy — same section**

An Outlier Town role with no active ability. Its value comes from discussion, voting, and information organization rather than nightly feedback.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is Town Outlier. Use Town Outlier or your exact role when the setup calls for it; do not invent a universal acronym for an Outlier.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

You have no special night ability. Your useful N1 work is reading claims and preparing to compare D2 evidence. No target is required.

**Expanded copy — same section**

You have no special night ability. Your useful N1 work is reading claims and preparing to compare D2 evidence.

**How to choose:** No target is required. With no D1 information, leave the suspect list open; neither silence nor having no button means you must invent an investigative story.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Keep an honest action history. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Log no role action and read the graveyard and TI posts.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Log no role action and read the graveyard and TI posts. You have not personally cleared anyone by surviving.

**What this does and does not establish:** No action supplies no investigative result. Your social reads should remain visibly separate from claimed mechanical facts.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim Pilgrim or Town Outlier when requested, consistent with the setup.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim Pilgrim or Town Outlier when requested, consistent with the setup. Contribute a concrete observation or question; your role’s lack of ability is not a reason to fabricate results.

Your actual alignment is Town Outlier. Use Town Outlier or your exact role when the setup calls for it; do not invent a universal acronym for an Outlier.

Claim the role or alignment based on the known deck. A legal uncounterclaimed slot can support credibility but does not universally prove Town.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

You still have no night action.

**Expanded copy — same section**

You still have no night action. Update your notes with claim changes, votes, and contradictions so you can help choose a supported vote on the next day.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Learn the deck, claims, and voting rhythm.
- **Middle:** Ask specific questions and organize contradictions honestly.
- **Late:** Explain vote choices from public evidence; do not invent night proof to compensate for lacking an ability.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not fabricate results as the beginner default. Do not claim the role in an ineligible setup. Do not treat silence as helpful play.

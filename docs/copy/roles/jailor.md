# Jailor: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TPow — Town Power.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Select a prisoner during the day, question them privately at night, and optionally execute from N2. Three executions; killing Town removes further abilities. TPow — Town Power.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Select a prisoner during the day, question them privately at night, and optionally execute from N2. Three executions; killing Town removes further abilities.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Choose your prisoner during D1 for N1. Ask for their exact role, intended action, and the reason for it; you cannot execute on N1. Choose an unresolved player whose answer you can follow up tomorrow.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose your prisoner during D1 for N1. Ask for their exact role, intended action, and the reason for it; you cannot execute on N1.

**How to choose:** Choose an unresolved player whose answer you can follow up tomorrow. With no useful D1 evidence, choose an eligible unknown rather than treating silence or a player number as guilt. Write down the choice before night starts so you do not miss the day selection.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Get one usable prisoner claim. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record the prisoner, their claim, and anything they actually said.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record the prisoner, their claim, and anything they actually said. An unconvincing answer is a lead, not a guilty result. A death-free night does not prove you jailed the killer.

**What this does and does not establish:** Jail blocks ordinary direct visits but has exceptions. No kill while someone is jailed is a clue rather than unique proof.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

If needed, claim TPow without publishing tomorrow’s prisoner.

**Expanded copy — same section**

If needed, claim TPow without publishing tomorrow’s prisoner. Ask a precise question when the prisoner’s account conflicts with public evidence. Sharing the Jailor identity privately requires a reason to trust the recipient.

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Consider how public identity affects survival. Use alignment-first disclosure where appropriate; reveal when coordination or defense requires it.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Choose a different prisoner because consecutive jails are restricted.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose a different prisoner because consecutive jails are restricted. If D2 supplies a supported evil case, consider execution once enabled; otherwise question a new unresolved player. Check protection and remaining executions rather than assuming an execution must kill.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Question unresolved players and preserve a complete private log.
- **Middle:** Execute supported evils while accounting for dangerous prisoner types.
- **Late:** Count votes and remaining charges; the wrong execution can remove both your power and a needed Town vote.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Keep a truthful Jailor history. You may browse or select any claim; recommendations do not lock the picker.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Consider how public identity affects survival. Use alignment-first disclosure where appropriate; reveal when coordination or defense requires it.

A genuine Town player normally needs truthful disclosure rather than a fabricated investigation. Choosing a different public role creates that role’s obligations without giving you its ability. Read the selected cover’s exact action timing and evidence limits before using it. The all-role selector remains available even when an option is difficult or unsupported.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not execute N1. Do not repeat the same prisoner on consecutive nights. Do not rely on jail to stop every astral effect.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not execute N1. Do not repeat the same prisoner on consecutive nights. Do not rely on jail to stop every astral effect.

**Important interaction:** Execution is Powerful, not universally unstoppable. Some astral protection can save a prisoner; dangerous prisoners can retaliate.

**Worked decision:** D2 select #7; N2 compare their exact claim with D2 statements before choosing whether to execute.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Get one usable prisoner claim.
2. Compare it with independent D2 evidence.
3. Use execution on evidence, not to punish a nervous answer.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Get one usable prisoner claim.
2. Compare it with independent D2 evidence.
3. Use execution on evidence, not to punish a nervous answer.

- Choose unresolved players whose claims can be tested.
- Ask for role, will, and reasoning before deciding.
- Reserve executions for supported cases and changing vote control.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

## Will notes — lower priority

**Compact copy**

D jail selection; N prisoner; claimed role; release/execute; actual outcome.

**Expanded copy — same section**

For a truthful Town draft, include only actions/results you actually performed or received.

**Fields:** D jail selection; N prisoner; claimed role; release/execute; actual outcome.

**Illustrative formatting only — not live data:**

```text
D2: Select 7
N2: Jail 7 — [claim]; released
```

No N1 execution, no consecutive prisoner. Three executions; lost abilities must alter later input options.

# Monarch: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TPow — Town Power.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Two Knight charges grant extra votes. A living Knight grants you Basic Defense after the knighting resolves; a Knight need not be Town. TPow — Town Power.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Two Knight charges grant extra votes. A living Knight grants you Basic Defense after the knighting resolves; a Knight need not be Town.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

You can Knight an eligible player, but holding a charge on low-information N1 is a reasonable beginner choice. Knight someone only when you have a concrete reason to trust their judgment or faction.

**Expanded copy — same section**

You can Knight an eligible player, but holding a charge on low-information N1 is a reasonable beginner choice.

**How to choose:** Knight someone only when you have a concrete reason to trust their judgment or faction. If everyone is unknown, conserve one of your two charges rather than granting an enemy extra voting power.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Preserve charges without trust. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record whether the knighting resolved and the remaining charges.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record whether the knighting resolved and the remaining charges. A Knight is not automatically Town, and the associated defense should not be assumed to have existed before the action resolved.

**What this does and does not establish:** A visible Knight demonstrates knighting occurred, not who performed it without other evidence, nor the Knight’s loyalty.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim TPow when requested.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TPow when requested. Explain a knighting when it matters to voting, but separate “I gave this player power” from “this player is confirmed good.”

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Claim carefully; use the Knight history to explain your role when needed. Being Knighted is not allegiance confirmation.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

If D2 establishes a useful ally, consider a first or second Knight.

**Expanded copy — same section**

If D2 establishes a useful ally, consider a first or second Knight. If the first Knight looks suspicious, do not spend the next charge to reinforce that mistake. Check who benefits from the extra vote.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Prioritize confidence in allegiance before giving permanent votes.
- **Middle:** Use Knights strategically and monitor their survival.
- **Late:** Calculate how added vote weight changes control rather than treating knighting as an automatic confirmation.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Keep a truthful Monarch history. You may browse or select any claim; recommendations do not lock the picker.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim carefully; use the Knight history to explain your role when needed. Being Knighted is not allegiance confirmation.

A genuine Town player normally needs truthful disclosure rather than a fabricated investigation. Choosing a different public role creates that role’s obligations without giving you its ability. Read the selected cover’s exact action timing and evidence limits before using it. The all-role selector remains available even when an option is difficult or unsupported.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not knight an impressive speaker solely on confidence. Do not assume Knights are unvoteable. Do not assume a future Knight saves you tonight.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not knight an impressive speaker solely on confidence. Do not assume Knights are unvoteable. Do not assume a future Knight saves you tonight.

**Important interaction:** Charges are not spent if the selected player dies before knighting completes. Corpse users can reproduce knighting effects.

**Worked decision:** #4 has strong independent Town evidence: increasing their vote is more defensible than rewarding an unverified TPow claim.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Preserve charges without trust.
2. Empower an independently supported ally.
3. Track how Knights change the vote.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Preserve charges without trust.
2. Empower an independently supported ally.
3. Track how Knights change the vote.

- Knight players with strong faction evidence.
- Consider vote consequences before adding weight.
- Track living Knights and do not assume immediate first-night defense.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

## Will notes — lower priority

**Compact copy**

Knight target; attempted/completed/failed; current living Knights; charges.

**Expanded copy — same section**

For a truthful Town draft, include only actions/results you actually performed or received.

**Fields:** Knight target; attempted/completed/failed; current living Knights; charges.

**Illustrative formatting only — not live data:**

```text
N1: Knight 4 — completed D2
```

Two charges. Failed/dead-target attempts do not automatically consume a charge. Knights do not prove faction.

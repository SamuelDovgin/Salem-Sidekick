# Socialite: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TS — Town Support.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Add up to two guests over day actions, then Party at a house to reject uninvited eligible visitors. Cannot party at the same house consecutively. TS — Town Support.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Add up to two guests over day actions, then Party at a house to reject uninvited eligible visitors. Cannot party at the same house consecutively.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TS — Town Support. When the lobby asks for alignment, TS is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Check the Guest List and Party options. A Party affects access to a house; it is not an ordinary roleblock of that house’s owner. Use available daytime invitations deliberately, at the rate the client permits.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Check the Guest List and Party options. A Party affects access to a house; it is not an ordinary roleblock of that house’s owner.

**How to choose:** Use available daytime invitations deliberately, at the rate the client permits. With no trusted guests or useful house plan, holding can avoid excluding needed Town visitors. If partying, choose a house where the access restriction has a reason.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Track invitations separately. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record the Party house, guest list state, and actual rejection feedback.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record the Party house, guest list state, and actual rejection feedback. Learning that someone was rejected does not give you a complete list of visitor identities.

**What this does and does not establish:** You learn whether someone was rejected, not a complete list of identities. A blocked visit is an obstacle, not ordinary roleblocking.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim TS if asked.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TS if asked. Coordinate invitations and access when it helps a supported ally; avoid promising every protective can enter. A daytime invite must be logged as a day action.

Your actual alignment is TS — Town Support. When the lobby asks for alignment, TS is your default shorthand.

Claim TS where appropriate. Full claim when party coordination or a blocked ally makes the distinction important.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Choose a different house if partying again because consecutive houses are restricted.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose a different house if partying again because consecutive houses are restricted. Use D2 claims to update invitations and decide whether access control is useful or would obstruct allies.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Invite allies whose access is likely to matter.
- **Middle:** Coordinate alternating party houses without blocking valuable Town actions.
- **Late:** Keep the necessary surviving allies able to visit and vote; guest-list access is not faction proof.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Keep a truthful Socialite history. You may browse or select any claim; recommendations do not lock the picker.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TS where appropriate. Full claim when party coordination or a blocked ally makes the distinction important.

A genuine Town player normally needs truthful disclosure rather than a fabricated investigation. Choosing a different public role creates that role’s obligations without giving you its ability. Read the selected cover’s exact action timing and evidence limits before using it. The all-role selector remains available even when an option is difficult or unsupported.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not automatically invite rejected visitors. Do not repeat the same party house. Do not report identities you did not receive.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not automatically invite rejected visitors. Do not repeat the same party house. Do not report identities you did not receive.

**Important interaction:** Party immunity and astral visits matter. Poisoner smog differs from Party and can block a real Socialite.

**Worked decision:** D2 invite #7; N2 party #4: #7 may access it while other uninvited normal visitors are rejected.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Track invitations separately.
2. Party for a clear access-control purpose.
3. Adjust the next house and guest plan.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Track invitations separately.
2. Party for a clear access-control purpose.
3. Adjust the next house and guest plan.

- Invite trusted allies whose visits need access.
- Choose a useful party target without sabotaging allied actions.
- Track guest-list additions separately from party nights.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

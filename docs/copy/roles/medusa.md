## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Stonemark an eligible player if doing so has a useful concealment purpose. You have three active-use charges; starting self-Stone is not an extra chosen target. Prefer a target whose later hidden death information helps the team, and coordinate with likely kills.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Stonemark an eligible player if doing so has a useful concealment purpose. You have three active-use charges; starting self-Stone is not an extra chosen target.

**How to choose:** Prefer a target whose later hidden death information helps the team, and coordinate with likely kills. With no concrete plan, holding a charge is reasonable. The mark persists; you do not need to spend one nightly by habit.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Choose a useful concealment target. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record the applied mark or failure and remaining charges.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record the applied mark or failure and remaining charges. A mark does not kill the target immediately; the book adds a distinct attack effect.

**What this does and does not establish:** Coven may learn information opponents cannot read. It should not appear in a claimed Town will without a legitimate explanation.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Reassess living marked players and expected deaths.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Reassess living marked players and expected deaths. Mark another useful target or hold; with the book, account for the attack and its visible consequences in the cover story.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Suppress meaningful information rather than marking random expendable players.
- **Middle:** Use hidden information to plan without leaking it through an incompatible cover.
- **Late:** Retain role-space uncertainty where useful, but account for true-role recovery by other abilities.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Psychic (TI): visiting fallback; Cleric (TP): visiting fallback. All roles remain selectable.

**Expanded copy — same section**

**Psychic — TI (Town Investigative); Visiting fallback.** A direct Stonemark can fit a one-player visit. **What can expose it:** There is no genuine vision; persistent marks and hidden-death information can expose the plan.

**Cleric — TP (Town Protective); Visiting fallback.** A selected player can match a reported Barrier destination. **What can expose it:** Stonemark does not protect them, and a later book kill changes the consequences.

These are starting explanations, not a whitelist. Choose any role from the full roster, including a weak fit, and read that claim’s real obligations inline. Matching one visible visit is weaker than matching movement, result scope, target feedback, and later demonstration together. Keep actual actions private and deliberately author the public cover; do not treat invented cover text as a genuine game result.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not use the old stay-home visitor-petrification ability. Do not assume Stone kills. Do not assume hidden bodies prevent every role from learning their truth.

**Important interaction:** Necromancer can use Stoned bodies; normal Town Retributionist cannot. Amnesiac can remember underlying roles.

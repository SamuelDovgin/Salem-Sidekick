# Trapper: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TP — Town Protective.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Build before placing a trap. One trap can remain active; a trigger can reveal visitor roles or intercept a harmful visitor. Dismantling rebuilds for a later placement. TP — Town Protective.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Build before placing a trap. One trap can remain active; a trigger can reveal visitor roles or intercept a harmful visitor. Dismantling rebuilds for a later placement.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TP — Town Protective. When the lobby asks for alignment, TP is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Build your trap on N1. A fresh Trapper cannot place an unbuilt trap. There is no numbered placement choice yet.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Build your trap on N1. A fresh Trapper cannot place an unbuilt trap.

**How to choose:** There is no numbered placement choice yet. Use D1 claims to shortlist a later house, but do not write “Trap on [player]” for N1 merely because you intend to place it there tomorrow.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Complete the build. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record whether the build completed.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record whether the build completed. If blocked or interrupted, the trap may still be unready; read the actual feedback before planning a placement.

**What this does and does not establish:** Your later trap feedback can concern a placement from an earlier night. Do not assign a visitor role to a player without independent evidence.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim TP when requested.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TP when requested. Explain “N1: Build” if asked for your action. Do not claim a trap trigger or a save before a trap has actually been placed and triggered.

Your actual alignment is TP — Town Protective. When the lobby asks for alignment, TP is your default shorthand.

Claim TP first where useful; full claim with a coherent trap timeline when needed. A trigger result reports roles rather than a Lookout-style numbered list.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

If built, place the trap at a credible useful player likely to attract relevant visitors.

**Expanded copy — same section**

If built, place the trap at a credible useful player likely to attract relevant visitors. If not built, complete the build first. Keep the one-active-trap limit and later trigger/rebuild sequence visible.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Build and keep an accurate state log.
- **Middle:** Place on a player whose protection or visitor roles would matter.
- **Late:** Preserve a useful existing trap or justify dismantling; do not reset its history to fit a narrative.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not place N1 as though already built. Do not keep two active traps. Do not claim a trigger while still rebuilding.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not place N1 as though already built. Do not keep two active traps. Do not claim a trigger while still rebuilding.

**Important interaction:** Blocks can prevent building. Astral actions do not trigger the trap; some harmful visits and overlapping interception require special handling.

**Worked decision:** N1 built; N2 placed on #4; N3 triggered: preserve that sequence instead of claiming a new trap every night.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Complete the build.
2. Make one legal placement.
3. Interpret the actual trigger before rebuilding.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Complete the build.
2. Make one legal placement.
3. Interpret the actual trigger before rebuilding.

- Build successfully before planning a placement.
- Trap a credible valuable player likely to draw informative traffic.
- Track built, placed, triggered, and rebuilding states explicitly.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

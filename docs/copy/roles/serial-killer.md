# Serial Killer: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: NK — Neutral Killing.** Default faction: Neutral. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Make night attacks, build Bloodlust toward a Powerful Rampage, and choose Cautious to suppress retaliation against disruptors. Normally roleblock immune. NK — Neutral Killing.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Make night attacks, build Bloodlust toward a Powerful Rampage, and choose Cautious to suppress retaliation against disruptors. Normally roleblock immune.

**Win condition:** Eliminate opposing factions until only your allied Serial Killer faction remains.

Your actual alignment is NK — Neutral Killing. This is private actual-role information. Your public alignment comes from the selected cover, not from this label.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

If enabled, choose an eligible nonally to attack and deliberately check Cautious mode. Grace Period can prevent an opening kill. Prefer a useful enemy with no obvious defense or visitor trap.

**Expanded copy — same section**

If enabled, choose an eligible nonally to attack and deliberately check Cautious mode. Grace Period can prevent an opening kill.

**How to choose:** Prefer a useful enemy with no obvious defense or visitor trap. With no evidence, choose an eligible unknown; a random legal attack is not an exact role test. Cautious changes retaliation behavior, so do not leave the toggle unexplained.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Make a legal attack with an intentional toggle. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record actual kills and Bloodlust state.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record actual kills and Bloodlust state. An attempted attack that fails does not grant the same progress, and no kill can reset the progression.

**What this does and does not establish:** Killing a target does not let you claim arbitrary Town attack feedback. Target defense can reset progression.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Use D2 role claims and actual defense feedback to choose a useful next victim.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Use D2 role claims and actual defense feedback to choose a useful next victim. Recheck Cautious and Bloodlust rather than assuming last night’s setting or attack power guarantees the next result.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Choose killable targets and an intelligible cover.
- **Middle:** Track Bloodlust and set Cautious deliberately.
- **Late:** Keep enough credibility for the last vote, not merely tonight’s kill.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Cleric (TP): visiting fallback; Vigilante (TK): attack story; difficult timing. All roles remain selectable.

**Expanded copy — same section**

**Cleric — TP (Town Protective); Visiting fallback.** An ordinary attack shares a single-player destination. **What can expose it:** No Barrier exists, and deaths/retaliation expose a different effect.

**Vigilante — TK (Town Killing); Attack story; difficult timing.** Later direct kills can be described as a shooting story. **What can expose it:** A real Vigilante cannot shoot N1 and needs Load/ammo history; attack labels and Bloodlust differ.

These are starting explanations, not a whitelist. Choose any role from the full roster, including a weak fit, and read that claim’s real obligations inline. Matching one visible visit is weaker than matching movement, result scope, target feedback, and later demonstration together. Keep actual actions private and deliberately author the public cover; do not treat invented cover text as a genuine game result.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not assume ordinary roleblocking stops you. Do not forget to set Cautious intentionally. Do not claim the special counterattack power as ordinary basic damage.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not assume ordinary roleblocking stops you. Do not forget to set Cautious intentionally. Do not claim the special counterattack power as ordinary basic damage.

**Important interaction:** Sources describe retaliation through natural defense with protection exceptions; exact attack-label modeling is disputed and excluded from hard rules.

**Worked decision:** A roleblocker is likely to visit: choose whether Cautious better preserves your cover before the night resolves.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Make a legal attack with an intentional toggle.
2. Track real kill progression.
3. Adapt to defense and retaliation risks.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Make a legal attack with an intentional toggle.

3. Adapt to defense and retaliation risks.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

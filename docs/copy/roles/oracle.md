# Oracle: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TP — Town Protective.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Choose a Town role, not a player number, and grant its holders Powerful Defense astrally. No consecutive repeat role; Oracle itself is a once-only choice. TP — Town Protective.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose a Town role, not a player number, and grant its holders Powerful Defense astrally. No consecutive repeat role; Oracle itself is a once-only choice.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TP — Town Protective. When the lobby asks for alignment, TP is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Choose a role for Aegis, rather than a numbered player, if the control is available. Grace Period can disable N1 Aegis. Use a role you have a concrete reason to believe is present and threatened.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose a role for Aegis, rather than a numbered player, if the control is available. Grace Period can disable N1 Aegis.

**How to choose:** Use a role you have a concrete reason to believe is present and threatened. If the role list gives no lead, weigh the known presence of your own role against spending its once-only self-role option; holding can be better than an uninformed guess.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Choose a role, not a player. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Record the selected role and actual feedback.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Record the selected role and actual feedback. You did not ordinarily visit every holder, and a lack of feedback does not reveal whether that role exists.

**What this does and does not establish:** A save does not necessarily tell you the saved player’s identity. The selected role and a supposed target number must not be conflated.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim TP if asked.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TP if asked. Explain Oracle when the role-targeted protection matters, without inventing a numbered Barrier visit or claiming that Aegis identifies every protected player.

Your actual alignment is TP — Town Protective. When the lobby asks for alignment, TP is your default shorthand.

Claim TP when pressured, then explain role-based choices if full claiming. Coordinate privately only with appropriately trusted players.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Choose a different legal role because consecutive role choices are restricted.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Choose a different legal role because consecutive role choices are restricted. Use D2 role evidence and threats to decide; if no useful legal role is supported, hold rather than invent a guaranteed target.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Use credible known role information rather than unsupported precise player guesses.
- **Middle:** Plan alternate Aegis choices around the no-repeat restriction.
- **Late:** Read which remaining roles are actually threatened; do not promise consecutive coverage you cannot provide.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Keep a truthful Oracle history. You may browse or select any claim; recommendations do not lock the picker.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TP when pressured, then explain role-based choices if full claiming. Coordinate privately only with appropriately trusted players.

A genuine Town player normally needs truthful disclosure rather than a fabricated investigation. Choosing a different public role creates that role’s obligations without giving you its ability. Read the selected cover’s exact action timing and evidence limits before using it. The all-role selector remains available even when an option is difficult or unsupported.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not write normal visiting protection wills. Do not repeat a role on consecutive nights. Do not claim precise player feedback the game did not supply.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not write normal visiting protection wills. Do not repeat a role on consecutive nights. Do not claim precise player feedback the game did not supply.

**Important interaction:** Aegis is astral and can bypass jail. An ordinary visible visit conflicts with an Oracle story unless a role/state change explains it.

**Worked decision:** You know a threatened Seer exists and did not Aegis Seer last night: selecting that role can cover them without a direct visit.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Choose a role, not a player.
2. Respect consecutive and self-role restrictions.
3. Update protection from actual role evidence.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Choose a role, not a player.
2. Respect consecutive and self-role restrictions.
3. Update protection from actual role evidence.

- Use credible exact-role information to choose Aegis.
- Plan around the no-repeat restriction.
- Prefer protection with genuine expected value rather than guessing a famous role.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

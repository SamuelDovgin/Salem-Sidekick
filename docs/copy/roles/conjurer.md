## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

You cannot Meteor on D1. At night, use the book attack only if you hold it and the setup enables it; otherwise prepare your cover and later day target. With no book, there is no extra N1 attack to invent.

**Expanded copy — same section**

You cannot Meteor on D1. At night, use the book attack only if you hold it and the setup enables it; otherwise prepare your cover and later day target.

**How to choose:** With no book, there is no extra N1 attack to invent. Note a possible later high-value enemy, then wait for evidence. With the book, coordinate a legal nonally target and the team’s other visits.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Keep day and night actions distinct. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Log the actual book action or no role action.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Log the actual book action or no role action. Your future Meteor is a separate day ability and does not explain an ordinary N1 death by itself.

**What this does and does not establish:** A public meteor announces the ability occurred, not which living player used it.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Recheck book ownership.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Recheck book ownership. Save or use the later day Meteor for a concrete faction benefit, then keep its charge state distinct from night attacks. Do not claim a second Meteor after spending it.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Keep the meteor until a valuable opportunity.
- **Middle:** Remove a consequential target when it changes control.
- **Late:** Do not die holding it merely out of habit; assess defense and the immediate vote state.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Oracle (TP): conditional nonvisit fit; Deputy (TK): difficult demonstration claim. All roles remain selectable.

**Expanded copy — same section**

**Oracle — TP (Town Protective); Conditional nonvisit fit.** Before holding the book, no ordinary night action can fit astral movement. **What can expose it:** You cannot provide Aegis; later direct book attacks alter the footprint.

**Deputy — TK (Town Killing); Difficult demonstration claim.** An inactive night is compatible with a real Deputy’s lack of night action. **What can expose it:** You cannot reproduce the Deputy shot. A Meteor is a different ability, not a successful Deputy demonstration.

These are starting explanations, not a whitelist. Choose any role from the full roster, including a weak fit, and read that claim’s real obligations inline. Matching one visible visit is weaker than matching movement, result scope, target feedback, and later demonstration together. Keep actual actions private and deliberately author the public cover; do not treat invented cover text as a genuine game result.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not assume night barriers persist into daytime. Do not claim a public reveal ability you cannot demonstrate. Do not use the meteor on an invulnerable target casually.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not assume night barriers persist into daytime. Do not claim a public reveal ability you cannot demonstrate. Do not use the meteor on an invulnerable target casually.

**Important interaction:** The meteor has different strength and feedback from a basic shot. Secret Killers does not justify assuming every cinematic disappears.

**Worked decision:** A confirmed leader threatens a decisive vote: assess whether meteor removal changes the outcome before spending it.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Keep day and night actions distinct.
2. Identify a useful later Meteor target.
3. Track the single day charge.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Keep day and night actions distinct.

Salem Sidekick — One-Page Beginner Strategy Guide
I'd build this as a compact, role-based strategy companion for Town of Salem 2. The goal is that you can select any role and understand how to play it in about 15–30 seconds, without needing to read the wiki during a match.

The most important difference from an ordinary role guide is that it should answer what you should actually do, not just explain what your ability does.

4. A dedicated claim strategy card
I think this is one of the most valuable parts for a beginner.

Every role gets a Claim Strategy section that distinguishes an alignment claim from revealing the exact role.

Role category

General beginner approach

Town Investigative

Share results early. Exact role is generally safer to reveal because Ritualist and Doomsayer cannot guess TI roles.

Town Protective

Claim TP when needed; avoid unnecessarily revealing your exact role or protection target.

Town Power

Preserve your identity until revealing offers a clear advantage, or you're forced to defend yourself.

Town Killing

Claim TK initially when appropriate; avoid broadcasting limited-use or dangerous abilities.

Town Support

Claim TS as needed; decide exact disclosure by the ability and current threat.

Coven

Choose a believable Town claim with actions and a will you can maintain. Never claim Coven to untrusted players.

Neutral

Prioritize your specific win condition. Claim advice varies substantially by role.

These are defaults rather than hard rules. In particular, even a strong Town Investigative claim can make you a priority target for ordinary night attacks.

Eventually we could have a lightweight 15-player notebook with player number, claim, known visits, and status. It should be optional and not get in the way of the quick guide.

For version two, the website would remember these entries locally in your browser during the match, suggest follow-up questions, and optionally generate your will. No account should be required.

8. A few implementation decisions I'd make now
Start with vanilla Town of Salem 2, all current playable roles, sorted into Town, Coven, and Neutral, with alignment filters.

Use the exact game's role icons, not generic fantasy illustrations. The wiki has a dedicated 
image category
 containing ToS2 role artwork. We should collect the files once, optimize them, and review the game-art rights and attribution requirements before publishing.

Keep everything on one page. Role selection switches the content without navigating or reloading.

Include a copy-will control for each role with a short, correct template, not a huge prewritten essay.

Support fast searching and keyboard navigation. Ideally, type part of a role name and press Enter.

Keep mechanics separate from strategic advice. Mechanics can be verified against the wiki; strategy should be labeled as a recommendation.

Support game modes eventually. This matters because the March 2026 Flexible Factions update permits unusual faction assignments, and BetterTOS2 has different role mechanics. We shouldn't accidentally mix their guides.

9. Development plan
Version 1 — Quick Guide

Complete role database, icon picker, night priorities, claiming advice, fake-claim suggestions, common mistakes, and copyable wills. Static, very fast, and easy to maintain.

Version 2 — Result Interpreter

Select the ability result you received and get conditional advice, likely explanations, contradictions, and suggested questions to ask in chat.

Version 3 — Match Assistant

Optional 15-player tracker that connects night results, claims, deaths, and inconsistencies, then helps you decide what to investigate next.

The most important architectural choice is to store each role's guide as structured content, not hard-code individual pages. That way, we can improve a recommendation or update an ability without redesigning anything.

For the first version, I'd prioritize making every role genuinely useful within 15 seconds of selecting it. A beginner should immediately know who to target tonight, what to say tomorrow, which fake claims are believable, and which conclusions they should avoid jumping to. Everything more sophisticated can grow around that foundation.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Check the book first. Without it, ordinary Drink is the simpler opening: attempt to roleblock one useful nonally. Smog is a different ability, not another name for Drink. Coordinate to avoid blocking an ally or obstructing allied visits.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Check the book first. Without it, ordinary Drink is the simpler opening: attempt to roleblock one useful nonally. Smog is a different ability, not another name for Drink.

**How to choose:** Coordinate to avoid blocking an ally or obstructing allied visits. Prefer a suspected useful enemy night actor; with no role evidence, choose an eligible nonally away from obvious visitor traps. Drinking with an uncautious Serial Killer can be dangerous.

### A concrete Drink → cover → rotate sequence

**Setup:** You are Poisoner, actually CU. Another Coven member has the book. #4 is an eligible nonally; Coven has no plan requiring #4 to act. There is no known dangerous visitor bait. These are example facts, not facts the site knows about a live match.

1. **N1 mode:** Choose ordinary Drink on #4 for a simple disruption plan. If no enemy role is known, choose an eligible unknown nonally; the choice is not a prediction that they must be a TI or killer. Check team plans first.
2. **Private record:** Write `N1: Drink 4 — [actual feedback]`. If interrupted, keep that outcome. Do not fill “success” just because you pressed the ability.
3. **Public cover:** Select **Tavern Keeper — TS** from the unrestricted claim selector. This is logical because the target can genuinely experience a roleblock, which the cover can produce. If a full claim is requested, a compatible deliberately authored public history is `Tavern Keeper / N1: Drink 4`. Do not copy the private Poisoner heading or faction information.
4. **D2 interpretation:** If #4 reports being roleblocked, that supports the action account; it does not prove you are Tavern Keeper or Town. If they report a different effect, do not silently alter your own private action record to fit them.
5. **N2 after successful Drink:** #4 is Hungover. Choose a different useful eligible target, such as an independently identified enemy night actor. Do not promise to roleblock #4 on consecutive nights.
6. **Alternative mode:** Smog blocks incoming visits to the selected house with an obstacle effect; it does not simply stop the owner’s action. Coordinate because allied visits can also be obstructed. Socialite — TS becomes an explainable but difficult alternative, with guest-list and immunity differences. Keeping Tavern Keeper while claiming every Smog report was a roleblock creates a mismatch.
7. **Book branch:** If you receive the book, the direct attack also roleblocks. Do not plan a normal book attack plus Smog together. A dead target and a genuine roleblock effect can change how well the old cover survives.

**What the beginner can now answer:** Which button starts the simple plan? Drink. Who is a legal fallback? A nonally, after checking team interference and visiting danger. What alignment goes public? TS for the Tavern Keeper cover, not actual CU. Who next? A new eligible target after successful Drink. Is Smog the same effect? No.

**Why this cover has a reason:** It matches target feedback as well as an ordinary visit. **Why it is not guaranteed:** A real effect does not establish faction; mode changes, Hangover, the book, and independent observations can expose differences. Cleric remains selectable as TP, but supplies a weaker story because you do not actually Barrier and the target may report disruption.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

After a successful Drink, choose a different eligible target because of Hungover.

**Expanded copy — same section**

After a successful Drink, choose a different eligible target because of Hungover. Use D2 evidence to find a useful disruption. If choosing Smog, coordinate access and reconsider the cover; with the book, the attack also roleblocks but does not normally combine with Smog.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Choose a valuable Drink target and a consistent cover.
- **Middle:** Use Smog only when blocking access is better than direct disruption.
- **Late:** Account for book constraints and whether your prior cover still explains tonight’s action.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Tavern Keeper (TS): natural effect fit on drink; Socialite (TS): partial effect fit; difficult on smog; Cleric (TP): unrestricted but weak fallback. All roles remain selectable.

**Expanded copy — same section**

**Tavern Keeper — TS (Town Support); Natural effect fit on Drink.** Drink can create the same roleblock effect the target expects from a Tavern Keeper. **What can expose it:** Hungover prevents a repeat lock; Smog is an obstacle effect, and book attacks add lethal consequences.

**Socialite — TS (Town Support); Partial effect fit; difficult on Smog.** Smog and a Party can both obstruct access to a house. **What can expose it:** Socialite has invitations, different immunity rules, and a different legal house sequence; resemblance is incomplete.

**Cleric — TP (Town Protective); Unrestricted but weak fallback.** An ordinary direct visit can be written as a claimed Barrier destination. **What can expose it:** Drink or Smog feedback does not match pure protection, and you provide no genuine Barrier.

These are starting explanations, not a whitelist. Choose any role from the full roster, including a weak fit, and read that claim’s real obligations inline. Matching one visible visit is weaker than matching movement, result scope, target feedback, and later demonstration together. Keep actual actions private and deliberately author the public cover; do not treat invented cover text as a genuine game result.

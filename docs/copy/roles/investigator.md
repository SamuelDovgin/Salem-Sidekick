# Investigator: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TI — Town Investigative.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Investigate crimes: Murder reflects an attack that night; Trespassing reflects opposing-faction visiting history. No Crime is the absence of detectable crimes. TI — Town Investigative.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Investigate crimes: Murder reflects an attack that night; Trespassing reflects opposing-faction visiting history. No Crime is the absence of detectable crimes.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TI — Town Investigative. As TI, normally post your exact role and actual results from D2; an empty or failed N1 is still an honest history.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

Investigate one unresolved player and preserve the exact crime feedback rather than converting it into a guilty/innocent label. Prefer someone with a checkable action claim or conflicting behavior.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Investigate one unresolved player and preserve the exact crime feedback rather than converting it into a guilty/innocent label.

**How to choose:** Prefer someone with a checkable action claim or conflicting behavior. With no D1 lead, choose an eligible unknown outside obvious dangerous visitor bait. A random legal choice gives limited evidence, not a reason to accuse.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Preserve literal crime feedback. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Write Murder, Trespassing, No Crime, combined feedback, or an actual failed action exactly as received.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Write Murder, Trespassing, No Crime, combined feedback, or an actual failed action exactly as received. Murder concerns an attack that night; Trespassing concerns opposing-faction visiting history.

**What this does and does not establish:** A crime does not establish evil. Town attackers and cross-faction Town visits can explain crime. Murder and Trespassing are separate concepts; preserve the feedback actually shown.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Post “Investigator (TI), N1: [player] — [actual feedback].” Ask a crime-positive target for their action and destination.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Post “Investigator (TI), N1: [player] — [actual feedback].” Ask a crime-positive target for their action and destination. A Town attacker or a Town visit across factions can explain crime.

Your actual alignment is TI — Town Investigative. As TI, normally post your exact role and actual results from D2; an empty or failed N1 is still an honest history.

Full claim with the actual crime feedback, normally from D2. Separate your result from your accusation.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

Check a player whose D2 story can be tested, and compare the new crime with known actions.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Check a player whose D2 story can be tested, and compare the new crime with known actions. If blocked N1, say that rather than backfilling No Crime. Do not rely on the disputed Jailor execution interaction.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Use the shorter visit history to ask concrete questions about Trespassing.
- **Middle:** Compare crime with actual action histories and independent observations.
- **Late:** Avoid expecting accumulated Trespassing to distinguish everyone; prioritize contradictions and immediate attack evidence.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not use old blood-only templates. Do not invent crime for a nonvisiting action. Do not treat No Crime as a faction clearance.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not use old blood-only templates. Do not invent crime for a nonvisiting action. Do not treat No Crime as a faction clearance.

**Important interaction:** Enchant, Reap, Illusion, and Shroud complicate interpretation. The source has conflicting execution examples; avoid a hard Jailor-Murder rule.

**Worked decision:** #4 shows Trespassing and claims to have checked #9: compare both claims; either player or another earlier visit may explain the result.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Preserve literal crime feedback.
2. Ask for the corresponding action history.
3. Compare with independent observations.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Preserve literal crime feedback.
2. Ask for the corresponding action history.
3. Compare with independent observations.

- Check players with disputed action histories.
- Ask crime-positive targets for their actions and destinations.
- Compare against known attacks and other observations.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.

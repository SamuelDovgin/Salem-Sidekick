# Claim-specific example wills and upkeep

**Current full-will formats:** [All 64 N1-N7 example/blank wills](../copy/FULL_WILLS.md) supersede the shorter formatting examples below. Role-policy and mechanics references below remain background material.

**Current selector scope:** public claims are limited to the 29 Town roles. All 64 roles/states remain available as actual roles. Coven/Neutral formats below are retained as own-role reference material, not selectable public covers.

Current publication copy for the example section, replacing the will builder. All numbers and outcomes below are illustrative; these are not current-match findings. Genuine Town records real feedback. An opposing player deliberately authors a cover separately from their actual actions.

The 29 Town claims have individually audited upkeep, payoff, exposure, posting timing, examples, and maintenance tips. Other selectable roles show their own source-checked format and clearly identify open faction disclosure, rather than pretending an evil role name is an ordinary Town cover.

**Modifier check:** Grace Period disables all protective abilities on N1 and has additional role-specific restrictions; it does not block every N1 action. Check the game's Night 1 notice and the role's enabled controls before recording or claiming an action. See the [official R1.5.01 notes](https://www.townofsalem.io/town-of-salem-2-bug-fixes-patch-notes-r1-5-01/) and [R1.5.03 notice update](https://www.townofsalem.io/town-of-salem-2-updates-and-bug-fixes-patch-notes-r1-5-03/).

## Jailor

**What this role does:** Pick a prisoner during the day, then question them privately at night. Their normal night action is restricted by jail. From N2 you may execute, with three executions; killing Town removes further abilities. Do not repeat a prisoner consecutively.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a real jail conversation that your prisoner can confirm, not just a name in a will. There is no N1 execution.

**For example:** “N1: Jail 4 — claimed Cleric; released” means you privately questioned player 4, who said Cleric, and chose not to execute.

**TPow — Town Power; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Real private jail conversations and executions create substantial evidence and control. A fake claim assumes a direct interaction that another player can readily confirm or deny.

**Exposure:** A fabricated prisoner can dispute being jailed. Wrong timing, consecutive prisoners, an N1 execution, or incompatible execution outcomes expose the story quickly.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TPow when appropriate. Share real contradictions with Town, but avoid publishing an unnecessary future prisoner or treating a private recipient as trusted without evidence.

```text
Jailor
N1: Jail 4 — claimed Cleric; released
N2: Jail 7 — claimed Sheriff; released
```

- Choose the prisoner during the preceding day and record their exact claimed role and useful statements.
- N1 permits questioning but not execution. Do not jail the same person consecutively.
- Record released or executed explicitly and distinguish an attempted execution from its actual outcome.
- Track the three executions and any loss of abilities; absence of night kills alone does not identify your prisoner as the killer.

**Blank format:**

```text
Jailor
N1: Jail [player] — claimed [role]; released
N[later]: [release / execution and actual outcome]
```

A well-written will cannot replace the private jail event. Execution should follow evidence, not demonstration pressure. Suitable protection and dangerous prisoners also complicate attempted execution outcomes.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Jailor_(ToS2)).

## Marshal

**What this role does:** Reveal yourself during the day by starting a Tribunal. Players can be executed directly through nominations, normally up to two victims, with role reveals and wills delayed until it ends. You have two uses and no night ability.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a visible daytime event. An easy empty night log cannot substitute for demonstrating Tribunal when the situation calls for it.

**For example:** “D2: Held Tribunal” means you saved a use. It does not mean a Tribunal took place or that any suspect was proven evil.

**TPow — Town Power; Low upkeep.**

**Payoff:** No night targets or daily investigative results are required. A real Tribunal can change the voting day, but a fake claim cannot reproduce that conspicuous public action.

**Exposure:** The role has two charges and one use per day. Inability to demonstrate a justified Tribunal is the central weakness of an otherwise easy will.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TPow during alignment claims. Announce and use Tribunal when a supported multi-target voting plan justifies it; claiming the role is not itself a reason to spend the ability.

```text
Marshal
N1: No night ability
D2: Held Tribunal — no supported multi-target case
D3: Tribunal — 4, 9 executed
```

- Write No night ability instead of manufacturing nightly checks.
- Use a D entry for a real Tribunal and list the people actually executed.
- Do not label every Tribunal victim evil: execution is an event, not an alignment result.
- Track charges and avoid claiming D1 use or repeated same-day uses.

**Blank format:**

```text
Marshal
D[day]: [held / Tribunal — actual executed players]
```

The example lists two legal D3 executions, not confirmed evils or a required schedule. Minimal typing leaves severe public-demonstration pressure. Conversion can separate role demonstration from faction.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Marshal_(ToS2)).

## Mayor

**What this role does:** Publicly Reveal from D2 to gain extra voting weight immediately and more on later days while alive. You have no night ability; the visible reveal and votes are the important evidence.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a public Reveal you can actually perform. Writing Mayor in a will does not produce the reveal or extra votes.

**For example:** “D2: Remained hidden; D3: Revealed” records when your public power appeared. It contains no investigative results.

**TPow — Town Power; Low upkeep.**

**Payoff:** No nightly action history is needed. A real Reveal can create voting leverage, making the role easy to demonstrate when revealing is strategically worthwhile.

**Exposure:** A false claimant cannot reproduce the public Reveal. A claimed vote-weight change must match the actual setup and day history rather than an invented night result.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TPow when asked for alignment. Reveal when the extra vote value and survival plan justify it; do not reveal solely because a will example contains a D2 line.

```text
Mayor
N1: No night ability
D2: Remained hidden
D3: Revealed
```

- Write No night ability for quiet nights; Mayor does not investigate or visit a target.
- Record Reveal on the actual day it happens, never D1 in the normal setup.
- Keep observational vote notes distinct from ability results.
- After revealing, continue recording useful cases and votes rather than inventing another activated power.

**Blank format:**

```text
Mayor
D[day]: [hidden / Revealed]
Notes: [public observations]
```

Minimal typing leaves immediate Reveal pressure. Extra votes do not make a suspicion correct. A demonstrated Mayor ability also does not establish allegiance where conversion or custom factions apply.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Mayor_(ToS2)).

## Monarch

**What this role does:** At night, make someone a Knight to give them extra voting weight. You have two Knight charges. Having a living Knight gives you Basic Defense after the action resolves; a Knight can still be evil.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a real effect on the recipient, who may verify it. Holding a charge means no one was knighted that night.

**For example:** “N2: Knight 4 — completed D3” means player 4 received the Knight effect at dawn, not that you proved 4 is Town.

**TPow — Town Power; Moderate upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Knighting can produce a persistent visible voting effect and help trusted allies. Quiet holds are simple initially, but the promised Knights create later verification pressure.

**Exposure:** You have two Knight uses and cannot invent the recipient’s actual Knight effect. Timing, dead or failed targets, and the living-Knight history must remain coherent.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TPow during alignment claims. Explain completed Knights when relevant, while remembering that the Knight effect proves an ability occurred rather than proving the recipient is Town.

```text
Monarch
N1: Held Knight — no trusted recipient
N2: Knight 4 — completed D3
```

- Record each Knight attempt, its target, and whether the effect actually completed.
- Do not mark a selection as completed before the night resolves.
- Track the two charges from real outcomes; failed or dead-target attempts need the actual feedback.
- Keep who is still a living Knight separate from the original list, and do not claim instant defensive benefits before resolution.

**Blank format:**

```text
Monarch
N[night]: [held / Knight player — actual outcome]
```

The example illustrates conserving charges, not a mandatory N2 start. Recipients can verify Knight effects, while real Monarchs must assess allegiance before handing out permanent vote weight.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Monarch_(ToS2)).

## Prosecutor

**What this role does:** Publicly force a daytime hanging through Prosecute, bypassing normal judgment. You have two charges, at most one use per day, starting D2; prosecuting Town removes the remaining charges.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a visible prosecution ability. You have no night checks, and you cannot demonstrate this role merely by claiming a prosecution in text.

**For example:** “D2: Held Prosecute” means you saved the ability. “D3: Prosecuted 9” needs the actual public event.

**TPow — Town Power; Low upkeep.**

**Payoff:** No night action or dawn result is expected. A real prosecution can resolve a supported daytime case, but a false claim has severe public-demonstration pressure.

**Exposure:** The actual Prosecute event cannot be supplied by merely writing it. D1 use, use during Tribunal, too many charges, or incorrect aftermath conflicts with the role.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TPow under alignment claims. Use the ability only when the case justifies bypassing the normal vote, and explain the evidence rather than spending a charge to appear confirmed.

```text
Prosecutor
N1: No night ability
D2: Held Prosecute — insufficient evidence
D3: Prosecuted 9
```

- Write No night ability instead of an investigative or protective target.
- Use D entries for prosecutions, with the actual target and outcome.
- Track two total charges and at most one use per day; obey the live phase restrictions.
- After a Town prosecution, preserve the actual penalty and remaining ability state rather than copying an outdated rule.

**Blank format:**

```text
Prosecutor
D[day]: [held / Prosecuted player — actual outcome]
```

The example does not identify player 9 as evil. A false claim cannot reproduce Prosecute. For the real role, a supported hold beats spending merely to demonstrate identity.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Prosecutor_(ToS2)).

## Coroner

**What this role does:** First examine a dead body during the day to collect information about its killer; this is an Autopsy. Then check living people at night against the killer records you have collected.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You need a real usable autopsy before the related night comparison. An ordinary N1 has no corpse record yet, so no useful killer match is expected.

**For example:** “D2: Autopsy 6; N2: Examine 9 — no match” means 9 did not match your collected killer records, not that 9 is confirmed Town.

**TI — Town Investigative; Moderate upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Quiet before corpses exist, then durable killer records. Coven may know its book killer, but that supplies only part of the claimed process.

**Exposure:** An ordinary N1 examination without an autopsy, invented killer roles, or ignoring accumulated records can expose the claim. Long stretches without actionable findings also create social pressure.

**When to claim/post:** Give the legal N1 limitation when asked, then publish useful day autopsies and night examination results as they become available. Do not pretend you had information before receiving it.

```text
Coroner
N1: No autopsy available
D2: Autopsy 6 — killer role Poisoner
N2: Examine 9 — no match
```

- Write D2 autopsy separately from N2 examination; killer-role feedback arrives at night’s start.
- Retain all previous autopsies because later examinations use the accumulated collection.
- Use Match or No match within that collection; No match is not confirmed Town.
- Preserve a failed examination as a failure and avoid invented framing excuses for results those effects do not alter.

**Blank format:**

```text
Coroner
D[day]: Autopsy [corpse] — [killer role]
N[night]: Examine [player] — [match / no match / failed]
```

The example requires an actual corpse 6 killed by Poisoner. Killer-role transitions and Shroud need specific explanations. Empty autopsies delay verification pressure; they do not remove it.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Coroner_(ToS2)).

## Investigator

**What this role does:** Visit a person and check for crimes. Murder means an attack that night; Trespassing concerns visits across factions. No Crime means no detectable crime, not guaranteed innocence.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise crime feedback, not an exact role or a Town/evil label. Your visit and the reported crime need to fit the action history.

**For example:** “N1: 4 — Trespassing” reports a crime on 4; you still need their action history to interpret it. Town players can show this too.

**TI — Town Investigative; Moderate upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Crime reports can create useful questions about action histories. The format is short, but explaining the distinction between Murder and Trespassing takes more care than a Sheriff entry.

**Exposure:** Impossible crime claims on nonvisiting actions, wrong attack timing, or mismatched destinations weaken the story. Frames, Illusion, and Shroud also complicate real results.

**When to claim/post:** Full-claim Investigator with literal crime feedback early from D2; then ask the target for their action and relevant destinations. Separate the observed crime from your faction inference.

```text
Investigator
N1: 4 — Trespassing
N2: 7 — No Crime
```

- Use Murder, Trespassing, or No Crime as actually reported; preserve combined or unusual feedback verbatim.
- Murder concerns an attack that night, not an old kill or a generic evil read.
- Trespassing concerns opposing-faction visiting history, so Town can show it too; do not invent it for an action that never directly visits.
- Log a failed investigation separately from No Crime and keep your selected target consistent.

**Blank format:**

```text
Investigator
N1: [player] — [exact crime feedback / failed]
```

This cover needs a coherent crime and movement story. Neither No Crime nor crime-positive feedback establishes faction. Do not build an accusation around uncertain Jailor-execution interactions.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Investigator_(ToS2)).

## Lookout

**What this role does:** Watch one person’s house and learn which visible players visited that house. You learn visitor identities, not their roles. Visits described as astral are not visible to this observation.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You owe the watched house and the full visitor list the game actually supplied. One known visitor is not automatically the complete list.

**For example:** “Watch 4 — visitors 7, 9” means 7 and 9 visited player 4. It does not mean player 4 visited 7 and 9.

**TI — Town Investigative; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Can corroborate real visitors or expose contradictions directly. Strong apparent detail comes with a larger consistency burden than a one-target protective will.

**Exposure:** Missing a known visible visitor, adding an impossible visitor, self-watching, or treating astral movement as ordinarily visible can expose a false account.

**When to claim/post:** Normally give useful observations from D2. If briefly withholding a list to compare independent claims, preserve the complete original result and explain the timing rather than inventing it afterward.

```text
Lookout
N1: Watch 4 — visitors 7, 9
N2: Watch 11 — no visible visitors
```

- Write the watched house first, then who visited that house; this is the opposite direction from Tracker.
- Use all visible visitors the game reports. No visible visitors is a real result; roleblocked is no result.
- Do not normally watch yourself or claim that absence proves no astral action occurred.
- Do not fill a list solely from morning claims and call it an independent discovery.

**Blank format:**

```text
Lookout
N1: Watch [house] — [visible visitors / none / failed]
```

One verified visitor helps, but a fabricated complete list has many contradiction points. Partial knowledge from another ability must not be promoted into exhaustive Lookout observation.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Lookout_(ToS2)).

## Psychic

**What this role does:** Visit one person and receive a group of names that includes them. Odd nights, including N1: two names with at least one appearing Good (Town). Even nights: three names with at least one appearing Evil (non-Town). The vision does not say which person fits.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You owe a complete vision with the correct number of names each successful night, normally posted near day start. You cannot choose every name; only your visited player is your selection.

**For example:** “N1 Good: 4, 9 (selected 4)” means “I visited 4; at least one of 4 and 9 appears Good.” Either or both could fit, and misleading effects can distort appearances.

**TI — Town Investigative; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Creates immediate group leads, but requires a valid new vision and early posting each morning. Independent reports are more useful than lists assembled after everyone has claimed.

**Exposure:** Parity, group sizes, the selected target, movement, and later revealed factions can contradict invented visions. Matching a visit supplies no real vision.

**When to claim/post:** Full-claim Psychic and post genuine visions near day start, normally D2 onward. Explain missing or failed visions rather than inventing replacement results.

```text
Psychic
N1 Good: 4, 9 (selected 4)
N2 Evil: 2, 7, 11 (selected 7)
```

- Odd nights: Good vision with two names. Even nights: Evil vision with three names. Follow special client feedback in unusual late games.
- Mark the player you selected and include them in that night’s vision.
- A Good pair has at least one apparent good player; an Evil trio has at least one apparent evil player. Neither means exactly one.
- Keep earlier visions visible and compare them with later evidence; do not silently replace names after claims or deaths.

**Blank format:**

```text
Psychic
N1 Good: [selected], [other]
N2 Evil: [selected], [other], [other]
```

Psychic trades daily work for visible contribution. Invented groups are not genuine results despite correct formatting. Cleric needs less upkeep, but neither unsupported story becomes confirmed.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Psychic_(ToS2)).

## Seer

**What this role does:** Choose two eligible people and compare their apparent factions. Friends means they appear to share a faction; Enemies means they appear to differ. You normally visit both, and target-reuse restrictions apply.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You owe two legal targets and one comparison. The result does not identify either person’s exact role or tell you which enemy is evil.

**For example:** “4 + 9 — Enemies” means they appear to be on different sides. If 4 is independently trusted, that makes 9 more concerning; it is not proof by itself.

**TI — Town Investigative; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Friends/Enemies pairs can quickly organize a case, especially around an independently trusted anchor. The apparent payoff is high, but each pair commits to two players and a persistent target history.

**Exposure:** Reused or otherwise ineligible targets, an impossible pair result, or only one personal destination can expose the claim. Apparent faction effects can also distort genuine results.

**When to claim/post:** Normally full-claim Seer and post each real pair result early from D2. Explain which other evidence makes one member a useful anchor; the result alone does not identify the evil member.

```text
Seer
N1: 4 + 9 — Enemies
N2: 2 + 11 — Friends
```

- Use two distinct eligible players and retain a used-target history; follow the client’s reuse and revealed-target restrictions.
- Show Friends or Enemies exactly. A failed pair is not a usable comparison.
- Do not turn Enemies into both evil or Friends into confirmed Town.
- Check whether your actual movement can support two personal visits before selecting this as a cover.

**Blank format:**

```text
Seer
N1: [eligible player] + [eligible player] — [Friends / Enemies / failed]
```

Knowing two factions through another ability can help invent a result without reproducing two Seer visits. Extra actions or astral effects need specific explanations, not automatic guilt.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Seer_(ToS2)).

## Sheriff

**What this role does:** Visit one person at night and get Innocent or Suspicious. This is a limited suspicion check, not an exact-role or guaranteed-faction reveal; some enemies appear Innocent and effects can make a Town player look Suspicious.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a check on the person you visited and the exact result you received. A failed check gives no usable result.

**For example:** “N1: 4 — Innocent” means your check found no suspicion on 4. It does not mean 4 is confirmed Town.

**TI — Town Investigative; Moderate upkeep.**

**Payoff:** The result vocabulary is simple, and an independently posted check can focus discussion. Repeated Innocent entries are easy to write but provide limited independent credibility.

**Exposure:** Your visited target must match observer evidence. Suspicious is not available against every evil, and Innocent does not establish Town; frames and other effects matter.

**When to claim/post:** Normally full-claim Sheriff with the exact result from D2 near the start of each day. Ask for an explanation of Suspicious without treating your accusation as unanswerable.

```text
Sheriff
N1: 4 — Innocent
N2: 7 — no result; roleblocked
```

- Use the literal Innocent or Suspicious feedback, keeping failed checks separate.
- Choose an unresolved target and commit to that number before hearing everyone’s morning reports.
- Record roleblock or another interruption instead of converting no result into Innocent.
- Keep a reason for each target: a disputed claim or an unresolved vote is more useful than pretending silence proves evil.

**Blank format:**

```text
Sheriff
N1: [player] — [Innocent / Suspicious / failed]
```

A fake Sheriff needs both a result story and a visiting story. Repeated Innocent results create little independent value; correctly reporting a framed target does not establish your faction.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Sheriff_(ToS2)).

## Spy

**What this role does:** Put a lasting Bug on a person. It can later report certain evil visiting roles or effects involving them. Older Bugs can still give information after you place a new one.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You owe a placement history and any later Bug feedback. A reported role is not the visiting player’s number.

**For example:** “N1: Bug 4” records placement on 4. A trigger on that Bug during N3 belongs under N3, even though you placed it on N1.

**TI — Town Investigative; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Persistent Bugs can report distinctive effects over several nights and support later discoveries. That ongoing history gives value but creates more bookkeeping than a simple protective claim.

**Exposure:** Invented trigger roles, forgotten older Bugs, nonexistent visitor identities, and contradictory placement history are the main risks. A quiet night does not confirm the recipient.

**When to claim/post:** Normally full-claim Spy from D2 and share real useful trigger information promptly. Keep placement nights separate from later trigger nights so Town can follow how you learned something.

```text
Spy
N1: Bug 4 — no trigger
N2: Bug 7 — no trigger
```

- Record each new Bug target and keep earlier Bugs in the active history.
- Write the role or effect the feedback reports, not a visitor player number you were never given.
- Label the trigger night explicitly, even when the Bug was placed several nights earlier.
- No trigger and a failed Bug attempt are different outcomes; do not use either to clear a player.

**Blank format:**

```text
Spy
N1: Bug [player] — [actual feedback]
N[later]: Bug on [player] — [reported role/effect]
```

Older Bugs create continuing obligations. Preserve literal feedback at uncertain interaction edges. A teammate’s real information still needs a plausible Bug history before it supports this cover.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Spy_(ToS2)).

## Tracker

**What this role does:** Follow one person at night and learn where they visibly went. This follows their outgoing movement, rather than watching who came to their house. Astral actions can leave no visible visit.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise the tracked player’s destinations and a visit of your own to that player. A successful observation with no visible movement differs from being blocked.

**For example:** “N1: 4 -> 9” means you followed player 4 and saw them visit player 9.

**TI — Town Investigative; High upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Real destination information can verify an action account or expose a visiting contradiction. It is most convincing when recorded before the target announces where they went.

**Exposure:** The target’s destinations and your own visit to the tracked player must both fit. No visible visit does not prove that the target did nothing.

**When to claim/post:** Normally full-claim Tracker with each result early from D2. Compare the tracked player’s own account without silently changing your destination list to match it.

```text
Tracker
N1: 4 -> 9
N2: 7 -> no visible visit
```

- Write tracked player -> their destinations, not the list of people who visited them.
- Include multiple visible destinations when actually shown; corpse destinations remain valid references.
- Use No visible visit only after a successful observation. Use failed or roleblocked for missing information.
- If your information persists from an old ability, distinguish it from where you personally went tonight.

**Blank format:**

```text
Tracker
N1: [tracked player] -> [destinations / no visible visit / failed]
```

Real compatible tracking information helps. Guessing every destination is harder. Accurate information from persistent abilities still does not explain a contradictory personal visit on the current night.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Tracker_(ToS2)).

## Bodyguard

**N1 setup and cover:** If Grace Period is active, protective abilities are disabled on N1. Do not claim an N1 protective visit or make a visible real-role visit to support this cover. The example starts on N2; that does not mean your real role must skip every legal nonvisiting N1 ability.

**What this role does:** Guard a person against one direct harmful visitor. A successful interception cancels that harmful action and counterattacks the visitor, putting you in danger. This is an interception, not a general protective shield.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You promise a legal guard attempt, not a guaranteed save. Your own survival and any interception outcome must fit the feedback.

**For example:** “N1: Guard 4” means you tried to intercept a harmful visit to 4. It does not mean someone actually attacked 4.

**TP — Town Protective; Low upkeep.**

**Payoff:** One guard target per night makes routine recordkeeping simple. A real interception is meaningful, but a quiet protective history alone establishes little.

**Exposure:** You promise interception of a direct harmful visitor and a counterattack. Fake saves, incompatible survivals, or more than two self-protections are harder to explain than quiet guards.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TP under alignment claims. Full-claim when protection coordination, a save, or a contradiction makes the exact ability relevant; keep unnecessary future targets private.

```text
Bodyguard
N2: Guard 4
N3: Self Protect — use 1
```

- Write Guard and the selected player even when nothing was reported; choosing a target is not a save.
- Use Self Protect at most twice and remember it supplies Basic Defense rather than a normal guard on another player.
- Keep the destination aligned with any actual observed movement.
- Do not erase a dead guard target automatically: astral bypass or additional attacks can explain some deaths; ordinary unblocked interception claims must still fit the evidence.

**Blank format:**

```text
Bodyguard
N1: Guard [player] — [actual feedback, if any]
```

Low upkeep describes writing effort. Repeated alleged interceptions with no compatible harm or attacker evidence require explanation. A real Bodyguard can survive with suitable protection; survival alone is not disproof.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Bodyguard_(ToS2)).

## Cleric

**N1 setup and cover:** If Grace Period is active, protective abilities are disabled on N1. Do not claim an N1 protective visit or make a visible real-role visit to support this cover. The example starts on N2; that does not mean your real role must skip every legal nonvisiting N1 ability.

**What this role does:** Each night, visit one person and give them a protective shield for that night. The game calls this Barrier: it grants Powerful Defense, which stops Basic and Powerful attacks, but not Unstoppable attacks.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You are saying you visited and protected that person. You are not saying they were attacked or that you saved them unless you received supporting feedback.

**For example:** When Barrier is enabled, “N1: Barrier 4” means “I protected player 4 on the first night.” It does not mean “4 was attacked.”

**Setup check:** If Grace Period is active, Cleric cannot Barrier another player on N1. Check the game's Night 1 notice before claiming an N1 Barrier.

**TP — Town Protective; Low upkeep.**

**Payoff:** One nightly target and few result obligations make this simpler than Psychic. Genuine save feedback can build credibility; a quiet target list supplies little independent proof.

**Exposure:** You promise Powerful Defense without necessarily supplying it. Claimed saves, movement checks, a second Self Barrier, or an incompatible protected-player death can expose the story.

**When to claim/post:** Answer TP during alignment claims. Give the full role and existing history when pressed or explaining protection; quiet nights need no invented dawn announcement.

```text
Cleric
N2: Barrier 4
N3: Barrier 7
```

- Choose a plausible valuable recipient before night resolves; keep the number consistent with your actual observed visit.
- Write Barrier 4, not saved 4, unless the actual feedback supports a save.
- Use Self Barrier at most once. Do not promise a private protection plan to everyone.
- Check the graveyard before posting: a dead target is a red flag, not automatic proof. An interrupted action or Unstoppable attack can explain death; an unblocked ordinary lethal attack conflicts with a genuine Barrier. Keep the original target rather than rewriting history.

**Blank format:**

```text
Cleric
N1: Barrier [player] — [actual feedback, if any]
```

These are illustrative cover statements, not abilities the app performs. Low upkeep means less writing, not stronger evidence. Silencing your purported recipient still conflicts with a quiet protective story.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Cleric_(ToS2)).

## Crusader

**N1 setup and cover:** If Grace Period is active, protective abilities are disabled on N1. Do not claim an N1 protective visit or make a visible real-role visit to support this cover. The example starts on N2; that does not mean your real role must skip every legal nonvisiting N1 ability.

**What this role does:** Visit one person, shield them against Basic and Powerful attacks (Powerful Defense), and make a Basic attack against one random direct visitor to their house. This is called Fortify. A friendly Town visitor can be hit too; other visitors can survive.

**By claiming it, you mean:** You are promising BOTH protection for the resident and danger to one visitor. You do not get a complete visitor list or automatically learn whom you attacked.

**For example:** “N1: Fortify 4” means “I protected player 4 and could attack one person who visited 4.” It does not mean you attacked player 4 or everyone there.

**TP — Town Protective; Moderate upkeep.**

**Payoff:** Can explain protection and a dangerous visitor outcome in one role. Routine entries are short, but visitors and death causes create more verification pressure than a quiet Cleric story.

**Exposure:** You promise Powerful protection and an attack on one random direct visitor. An invented attacker identity, incompatible death label, or unsupported saved claim can unravel the account.

### Match review: Arsonist claiming Cleric during Grace Period

**Observed in the supplied screenshots:** On D2, player 8 was put on trial and claimed `ClearicN1: Barrier 9`, then added “sorry im new.” Another player immediately pointed out that Grace Period was active. The town convicted 8–2, and the reveal showed Arsonist. Player names are omitted here.

The decisive problem was the claimed action: Cleric cannot Barrier another player during Grace Period, so `N1: Barrier 9` could not have happened. This is a timing contradiction, regardless of whether Cleric is otherwise a plausible movement cover for an Arsonist’s direct Douse. The official R1.5.01 notes disable protective abilities during the first Grace Period night and specifically remove Cleric’s ability during Grace Period; the current [Cleric mechanics page](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Cleric_(ToS2)) also says other players cannot be Barriered during it. Later patch notes added a Night 1 notice for Grace Period, so checking the modifier or its notice is part of preparing the will. [R1.5.01](https://www.townofsalem.io/town-of-salem-2-bug-fixes-patch-notes-r1-5-01/) · [R1.5.03](https://www.townofsalem.io/town-of-salem-2-updates-and-bug-fixes-patch-notes-r1-5-03/).

**Would a full claim have been better?** Once you were on trial, yes: give the exact claimed role and a short, mechanically legal will. You did full-claim; that part was appropriate. The problem was the impossible N1 entry. If asked only for alignment before trial, answering TP can be enough; claiming the full role earlier is a situational choice, not a guarantee against being voted up.

A more coherent D2 response would have been:

```text
Cleric
N1: No other-player Barrier — Grace Period
```

If you survived, add an N2 Barrier entry only after N2 resolves. Do not write a planned target or outcome as though the action already happened. A spelling error is easy to avoid and can weaken presentation, but it was secondary to the mechanical contradiction. “Sorry, I’m new” alone does not answer the specific concern; briefly explain the legal timing and what action remains available.

**Takeaway:** check the setup before preparing a cover. Record the action the claimed role could actually take, and distinguish “no action available” from a visit, Barrier, or save. A simple fake claim with a legal empty night is stronger than a detailed will containing an impossible action.

**Payoff:** This is the role’s own action history, not an ordinary Town cover. Public disclosure is a deliberate situational choice.

**Exposure:** Under the normal-faction profile, this role name can disclose an opposing objective; it is not a quiet survival claim.

**When to claim/post:** Use this history privately unless you deliberately choose open disclosure. The selected role’s actual objective still applies.

```text
Serial Killer
N1: Attack 4; Cautious on — [outcome]
```

- Attack target; Cautious toggle; retaliation; actual Bloodlust state.
- Private. Preserve actual progress rather than assuming every attempt kills; special retaliation is not modeled as a normal basic shot.
- Keep intended action and actual outcome separate; only write feedback that belongs to this role.

**Blank format:**

```text
Serial Killer
[day/night]: [legal action and target] — [actual feedback]
```

Cautious does not cancel your selected attack. It trades away automatic counterattacks for discretion; neither setting is universally optimal. Record actual Bloodlust after resolution. Counterattack power has conflicting source descriptions, so do not rely on it to defeat a specific protected target.

Mechanics: [role source](https://townofsalem.wiki.gg/wiki/Serial_Killer_(ToS2)).

# Mayor: complete interface copy

**Actual alignment: TPow — Town Power.** Default faction: Town. Copy review: 2026-10-15.

The compact text and its expanded text belong to the **same section on the single page**. The expansion is additional explanation, not a second role page or reading drawer. Examples are illustrative and must never be inserted into live match fields.

## Your role and immediate job

**Compact copy**

Reveal from D2 to gain an additional vote immediately and more on subsequent days while alive. A reveal creates public identity and exposure. TPow — Town Power.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Reveal from D2 to gain an additional vote immediately and more on subsequent days while alive. A reveal creates public identity and exposure.

**Win condition:** Win with Town by eliminating opposing factions; the role’s ability is a tool toward that shared objective.

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Baseline: vanilla ToS2 text chat, normal faction assignments. An eligible target means one the current client permits. Check the role card, enabled controls, setup modifiers, and actual feedback before following a conditional action. Grace Period can suppress harmful opening actions. Unknown setup is not evidence that an action succeeded.

## First night: action and target

**Compact copy**

You have no N1 action and cannot reveal on D1. Prepare to compare D2 claims and keep your reveal for a useful voting decision. Note who makes a concrete claim and who simply repeats a push.

**Expanded copy — same section**

You have no N1 action and cannot reveal on D1. Prepare to compare D2 claims and keep your reveal for a useful voting decision.

**How to choose:** Note who makes a concrete claim and who simply repeats a push. If D1 offers nothing, leave the suspect list blank; do not manufacture a target to feel active.

**Your opening checkpoint:** Keep a clear evidence list. You do not need a perfect read of the lobby to begin; you need a legal action or a deliberate hold and an honest record of what happened.

## At dawn: record and interpret

**Compact copy**

Read the new information before deciding whether revealing would change a vote.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Read the new information before deciding whether revealing would change a vote. Surviving N1 does not confirm anyone who says they protected you.

**What this does and does not establish:** Extra votes do not establish that your social reads are correct. Custom faction/conversion rules require separate allegiance reasoning.

Keep intended action, actual feedback, and your interpretation in separate sentences. “No result” must not silently become a negative result, a successful save, or confirmation of another player’s faction.

## D2: what to say

**Compact copy**

Claim TPow when needed.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Claim TPow when needed. Reveal when the extra voting influence has a concrete purpose, such as resolving a supported case or breaking a dangerous vote; it also exposes your identity and changes communication/protection considerations.

Your actual alignment is TPow — Town Power. When the lobby asks for alignment, TPow is your default shorthand.

Use discretion before revealing; claiming your exact role alone is not equivalent to the public Reveal effect.

Private sharing needs its own trust reason: a loud claim, a friendly whisper, or your untested guess is not enough to confirm the recipient. Before sending a private plan, ask what is gained by sharing that detail now.

## N2 and later: next decisions

**Compact copy**

No night action is required.

**Expanded copy — same section**

No night action is required. Use the next day’s evidence to update the case list. If already revealed, coordinate around actual threats instead of assuming every protective claim guarantees safety.

**How priorities change:**

- **Early:** Assess whether revealing now produces a useful vote advantage.
- **Middle:** Coordinate with evidence-based Town pushes while accounting for exposure.
- **Late:** Recount actual weighted votes; do not wait until an unrevealable trial position.

Reassess from the actual outcome, not from the outcome you hoped for. If you are redirected, converted, retrained, or transformed, keep the previous action history and switch to the current legal role/state instructions.

## Claim choices and logical fit

**Compact copy**

Keep a truthful Mayor history. You may browse or select any claim; recommendations do not lock the picker.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Use discretion before revealing; claiming your exact role alone is not equivalent to the public Reveal effect.

A genuine Town player normally needs truthful disclosure rather than a fabricated investigation. Choosing a different public role creates that role’s obligations without giving you its ability. Read the selected cover’s exact action timing and evidence limits before using it. The all-role selector remains available even when an option is difficult or unsupported.

## Mistakes and important interactions

**Compact copy**

Do not assume the ToS1 fixed-vote model. Do not wait until on trial to reveal. Do not lead votes without checking evidence.

**Expanded copy — same section**

Do not assume the ToS1 fixed-vote model. Do not wait until on trial to reveal. Do not lead votes without checking evidence.

**Important interaction:** Town Traitor hunt affects further vote growth. Public identity can enable role guessers and direct killers.

**Worked decision:** Your reveal would prevent a supported Town vote from losing today: use the actual button before the trial starts.

A counterexample is a reason to narrow your conclusion. If an unresolved interaction matters to this decision, use the source note and the actual client feedback rather than turning it into a hard clearance or accusation.

## Your subgoals

**Compact copy**

1. Keep a clear evidence list.
2. Reveal for a specific vote.
3. Reassess vote control after each death.

**Expanded copy — same section**

1. Keep a clear evidence list.
2. Reveal for a specific vote.
3. Reassess vote control after each death.

- Judge whether vote strength changes today’s outcome.
- Reveal early enough to affect voting when necessary.
- Recount weighted votes after every death or status change.

These checkpoints describe useful decisions, not guaranteed outcomes. A correct hold, a failed action recorded accurately, or a revised case can satisfy the learning goal even when the match goes badly.
